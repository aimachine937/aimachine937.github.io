AI policy and the EU AI Act

Most SMEs use AI rather than build it. That keeps the requirements manageable. But there are requirements, and they already apply.

What applies to you as a user of AI

  • AI literacy: Businesses that use AI must take measures that support AI literacy among the staff who use it. The duty has applied since February 2025 and was changed in July 2026 into an obligation of effort. You do not have to guarantee a specific level, but you must be able to show what you did
  • Transparency: From August 2026, people must be able to tell when they are dealing with AI, such as a chatbot or AI-generated content. A customer-facing agent must make this clear
  • Prohibited uses: Certain uses of AI have been banned since February 2025. They rarely affect ordinary SMEs, but you should know where the line is
  • High risk: The heavy requirements for high-risk AI, for example in recruitment and credit scoring, are postponed to December 2027 and August 2028. If you use AI to assess applicants or employees, keep an eye on this

What a useful AI policy contains

  • Which AI tools are approved and which accounts must be used
  • Which data must not be shared, such as personal data, customer data and trade secrets
  • That a person approves anything sent to customers or used in decisions
  • Who to ask when in doubt, and how to report mistakes
  • How you document what you have done for staff AI literacy

The best policy is one or two pages long and actually used. A long document nobody reads documents very little.

Questions

Are we covered by the AI Act if we only use ChatGPT or Copilot?

Yes, as a user of AI. For most SMEs it comes down to AI literacy and transparency. The heavy requirements for high-risk AI rarely apply.

Who supervises?

National authorities supervise the AI literacy requirement. They gained supervisory powers in August 2026.

Is this legal advice?

No. We help with the practical side: policy, guidelines and documentation. If you need a legal assessment, talk to a lawyer.
